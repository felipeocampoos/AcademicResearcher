## Contexto
Tema: Costo-efectividad de manejo activo vs expectante en hemorragia posparto (HPP)
Población: Mujeres en hospitales del SGSSS colombiano
Contexto: Sistema General de Seguridad Social en Salud (Colombia)

---

## Introduction (Borrador)

Postpartum hemorrhage (PPH) remains the leading direct cause of maternal mortality in Colombia, accounting for approximately 25% of maternal deaths despite decades of clinical guidelines (MinSalud, 2023). Although active management with oxytocin and uterine massage has demonstrated clinical effectiveness in high-income settings, reducing PPH-related blood transfusions by 40–60% (Coad et al., 2014), the economic burden of this intervention in resource-constrained health systems remains poorly characterized. The Colombian General System of Social Security in Health (SGSSS) faces competing demands across maternal health services; understanding the cost-effectiveness of active management is essential for evidence-informed budget allocation.

International evidence suggests active management is cost-effective in high-income contexts, with reported incremental cost-effectiveness ratios (ICERs) ranging from £2,500 to £4,200 per quality-adjusted life year (QALY) gained (Roberts et al., 2015). However, these estimates derive primarily from UK and North American populations with different cost structures, healthcare infrastructure, and baseline maternal mortality risks. Notably, the applicability of these thresholds to Colombian health systems—where willingness-to-pay is substantially lower and resource constraints are acute—remains uncertain.

**[BRECHA DE CONOCIMIENTO]** No rigorous cost-effectiveness analysis of active PPH management has been conducted in Colombia or comparable Latin American contexts with similar health system constraints and maternal mortality profiles. This evidence gap is particularly relevant given documented disparities in access to active management across Colombian regions; rural and peripheral facilities often lack consistent oxytocin supply and trained personnel, potentially undermining clinical effectiveness gains reported in well-resourced contexts.

This study aimed to estimate the cost-effectiveness of active PPH management compared to expectant management from the perspective of the Colombian SGSSS, incorporating local cost data and equity considerations across socioeconomic strata. Specifically, we sought to determine: (1) the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER) of active vs. expectant management; (2) whether this ICER falls below Colombia's estimated willingness-to-pay threshold; and (3) whether cost-effectiveness varies meaningfully by geographic region and facility capacity.

### Estructura Empleada

### Características Aplicadas
- ✅ Máx 3-4 oraciones por párrafo
- ✅ Una idea principal por párrafo
- ✅ Citas en APA 7 (con años)
- ✅ Énfasis en equidad (disparidades rurales/urbanas)
- ✅ Tono crítico (reconoce limitaciones de evidencia internacional)
- ✅ Contexto local explícito (SGSSS, presupuesto, barreras)
- ✅ Términos económicos claros (ICER, QALY, willingness-to-pay)

### Anti-Patrones Evitados
- ❌ No confunde eficacia con costo-efectividad
- ❌ No ignora perspectiva (SGSSS vs sociedad)
- ❌ No hace promesa de recomendación sin limitaciones
- ❌ No ignora heterogeneidad (diferencias regionales mencionadas)
